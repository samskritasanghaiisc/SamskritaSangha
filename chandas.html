Prosody & Meter Analysis

छन्दः शास्त्रम्
Chandas in Sanskrit Poetry

Exploring matras, ganas, and classical meters studied across our text reading sessions.

Foundations

1. Introduction to Matras (मात्राः)

We will see about matras first before going to chandas.

सानुस्वारो विसर्गान्तो दीर्गो युक्तपर्श्च यः।
वा पदान्ते त्वसौ ग्वक्रः ज्ञेयः अन्यो मत्रिको लृजु।।

A Guru (Guru - Long / Gvakra) matra is assigned in the following 5 cases:

  • सानुस्वारः (Anusvara): If it has an anusvara (e.g., अं).
  • विसर्गान्तः (Visarga): If it has a visarga (e.g., अः).
  • दीर्गः (Long Vowels): Letters with long vowels like आ, ई, ऊ, etc.
  • युक्तपरः (Consonant Cluster): If the next letter is part of a compound consonant cluster, the preceding letter becomes guru.
  • पदान्तः (End of Pada/Line): The last letter of a poem line, even if it has a short vowel, is generally considered guru in poetic context.

Lruju (Laghu - Short): Any vowel/syllable that does not satisfy the Guru criteria is classified as Laghu.

Prosodic Units

2. Understanding Ganas (गणाः)

Before examining specific meters, we analyze three-syllable groups known as Ganas, memorized via the mnemonic:

य -मा -ता -रा -ज -भा -न -स -ल -गं
1   2   2   2   1   2   1   1   1   2

Tri-syllable Gana Classifications (1 = Laghu, 2 = Guru):

  • Yagana (यगण): 1-2-2 (Laghu-Guru-Guru)
  • Magana (मगण): 2-2-2 (Guru-Guru-Guru)
  • Tagana (तगण): 2-2-1 (Guru-Guru-Laghu)
  • Ragana (रगण): 2-1-2 (Guru-Laghu-Guru)
  • Jagana (जगण): 1-2-1 (Laghu-Guru-Laghu)
  • Bhagana (भगान): 2-1-1 (Guru-Laghu-Laghu)
  • Nagana (नगण): 1-1-1 (Laghu-Laghu-Laghu)
  • Sagana (सगण): 1-1-2 (Laghu-Laghu-Guru)

Example: Consider the three-syllable word पञ्चमं. The syllable split is 2-1-2, hence it belongs to Ragana (रगण).

Anushtup (अनुष्टुप्)

3. Sloka (श्लोकः)

An Anushtup verse generally has 4 padas (quarters), each with 8 letters, summing to 32 letters. To be classified strictly as a Sloka, it must satisfy:

श्लोके षष्ठं गुरुज्ञेयं पञ्चमं लघु सर्वदा。
द्वितीय तुर्ययो: ह्रस्वं सप्तमम् गुरु च अनयता।।
  • 6th letter of every pada is always Guru.
  • 5th letter of every pada is always Laghu.
  • In the 2nd and 4th pada, the 7th letter is always Laghu (Hrasva).
Classical Meter

4. Shardulavikreedita (शार्दूलविक्रीडितम्)

Shardulavikreedita is a 19-letter chandas per quarter. Defined by the mnemonic:

सूर्याश्वैर्मसजस्तताः सगुरवः शार्दूलविक्रीडितम्।

Here, Surya = 12, Ashwa = 7 (total 19 letters). The ganas follow the order: Magana, Sagana, Jagana, Sagana, Tagana, Tagana + 1 Guru (मसजस्तताः + सः + गुरवः).

11-Letter Meters

5. Indravajra (इन्द्रवज्रा), Upendravajra (उपेन्द्रवज्रा) & Upajaathi (उपजाति:)

These are 11-letter meters per pada:

  • Indravajra: स्यादिन्द्रवज्रा यदि तौ जगौ गः। (Tagana, Tagana, Jagana + 2 Gurus).
  • Upendravajra: उपेन्द्रवज्रा जगतजा ततो गौ। (Jagana, Tagana, Jagana + 2 Gurus). The only difference from Indravajra is the first letter.
  • Upajaathi: A verse with 11 letters per pada that mixes Indravajra and Upendravajra lines. (For instance, the second sloka of Lilavati is an Upajaati).
Overview

6. Other Meters & Matra Chandas

  • Trotaka (तोतकम्): 12-letter verses (4 Saganas - 112 / LLG), seen in Totakashtakam, Venkatesha Stotram, and Kalinga mardanam of Narayaneeyam.
  • Sragdhara (स्रग्धरा): 21-letter verses structured as (7+7+7).
  • Kusumamanjari: Seen in the Rasakreeda Stotram of Narayaneeyam.
  • Matra Chandas (मात्राछन्दसः): Unlike Akshara chandas where letter counts are fixed, Matra chandas fixes the number of matras. Example: Aryavruttam, which has 12, 18, 12, and 15 matras respectively across its 4 quarters.
